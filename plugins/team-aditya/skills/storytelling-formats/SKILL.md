# Storytelling Template Database

Source: Short Form Academy's Storytelling & Scriptwriting system. A library of 10 proven 1st-person-POV storytelling formats, each with a script skeleton, a "secret sauce" note on what separates an outlier from an average execution, and plug-and-play mad-lib templates.

## How to use this skill

1. Identify which of the 10 starting points matches what the creator has (a problem they solved, a lesson they learned, a dream they're chasing, two contrasting states, etc.).
2. Follow that format's Script/Storytelling Strategy as the section order.
3. Fill in the mad-lib templates with the creator's specifics — swap placeholders, don't leave them in, and don't force every optional beat if it doesn't fit.
4. Apply the "Secret Sauce" note for that format before finalizing — it's usually the difference between a generic version and one that performs.
5. If writing for a specific client/voice, run the drafted script through that client's voice skill (e.g. Signal's voice-guard or a client-specific voice profile) after applying this structure — this skill governs narrative shape, not voice.

## The 10 Formats

### 1. Hero's Journey (1st POV Problem → Solution)
**Use when:** the creator has a personal transformation arc — overcame a problem or challenge.
**Structure:** Hook → establish character & problem → failed attempts (exaggerate the problem) → introduce novel solution → explain why it worked → (optional) CTA.
**Secret sauce:** Get the viewer to emotionally relate to the pain first — if they feel what the creator felt, they're invested in the solution.

Templates:
1. INTRO: "X days/years ago I [problem]." / "I was a [role] struggling with [problem], and I had no idea how to fix it."
2. INFLECTION POINT: "Because of this I was [pain points/symptoms]." / "It got so bad that I [lowest moment]."
3. RISING ACTION: "I tried [failed #1], [failed #2], and [failed #3] — nothing worked." / "For [X months/years] I did everything they said: [common advice]. Still no results."
4. CLIMAX: "Finally after [X months/years] I figured out the one thing that actually worked: [solution]." / "Then I discovered [solution], and everything changed."
5. FALLING ACTION: "When I started doing this I [results/proof]." / "Within [timeframe] I went from [before] to [after]."
6. RESOLUTION (optional CTA): "Because of this I started [business/service] so I could help people like me achieve [dream result]." / "Now I help [audience] go from [pain] to [dream result]. [CTA]"

### 2. Personal Learning / Epiphany (1st POV Insight)
**Use when:** the creator wants to teach backward from a proven result — lead with proof, then teach.
**Structure:** Hook → establish desired result with proof → explain the process → (optional) contrast with the common/obvious solution → (optional) CTA.
**Secret sauce:** Needs strong visible proof (screenshots) for a "how did they do that" curiosity hook, AND a non-obvious solution — otherwise the result reads as a lucky outlier, not replicable.

Templates:
1. HOOK (PROOF): "I [impressive result] in just [timeframe] — here's exactly how." / "This one change got me [result]. Let me show you." / "[Screenshot/proof]. Everyone keeps asking me how I did this."
2. BACKSTORY: "Before this, I was [previous state] and I thought [old belief]." / "For [X months/years] I was stuck at [plateau] because I was doing [wrong approach]."
3. THE INSIGHT: "Then I realized [realization] — and that's what changed everything." / "The mistake I was making was [mistake]. The fix was [fix]."
4. BREAKDOWN: "Here's exactly what I did: First [step 1], then [step 2], and finally [step 3]." / "Step 1 is [x]. Step 2 is [x]. Step 3 is [x]."
5. CTA: "If you want the full breakdown, [CTA]." / "This is exactly what I teach inside [offer]. [CTA]"

### 3. About Me (Origin Story)
**Use when:** telling the backstory behind the creator's mission/brand — usually pinned to a profile.
**Structure:** Hook → normal-life context → epiphany/change moment → how it changed the go-forward path → (optional) CTA.
**Secret sauce:** Built for personal trust, not professional authority — infuse humanity, not credentials.

Templates:
1. INTRO: "Hi I'm [name], and [X months/years] ago I was a normal guy/girl, working as a [role], living a normal life." / "Before all of this, I was just a [relatable identity] doing [relatable activity]."
2. CONFLICT: "Well, all up until [life-changing event]." / "Then one day [trigger event] happened, and I couldn't ignore it anymore."
3. EPIPHANY: "Because of this I realized [epiphany]." / "That moment made me understand that [realization]."
4. CHANGE: "Which led me to [action/actions], and that leads me to today. Now I [dream result], and [positive side effect]." / "So I [bold action]. Fast forward to today — I've [current result] and [lifestyle change]."
5. PURPOSE: "[Dream result] changed my life, and I made it my mission to help [audience] go from [pain] to [dream result] too." / "Now everything I do is about helping [who] achieve [what] without [common pain]."

### 4. Before and After (Transformation Reveal)
**Use when:** there are two contrasting states to show, and the format can be almost entirely visual.
**Structure:** Hook → before state → brief tension/transition beat → after state (dramatic reveal) → (optional) time/effort context → (optional) CTA.
**Secret sauce:** The size of the gap between before/after drives watch time and shares. Sync the reveal to a beat drop or trending sound. Keep it to 15-30 seconds — the format doesn't need explaining.

Templates:
1. HOOK: "[X months/years] of [effort] in [X seconds]." / "This is what [subject] looked like before vs. after [action]." / "Watch this transformation."
2. BEFORE: "This is where I started: [before state]." / "[X months/years] ago, this was my reality: [before visual/metric]." (or no words, just show it)
3. TRANSITION: no script — a visual/audio cut synced to a beat, snap, or clap.
4. AFTER: "And this is [subject] now." / "[X months/years] later." (or no words, let the gap speak)
5. CONTEXT (optional): "[X months] of [effort]. Worth every second." / "All it took was [action] for [timeframe]."
6. CTA (optional): "Want to see how I did it? [CTA]" / "Follow for the full breakdown."

### 5. Goal / Dream Journey
**Use when:** the creator has an ongoing, open-ended aspiration (not a bounded challenge) and wants the audience to follow along.
**Structure:** Hook → introduce the dream and its origin → the moment you decided to seriously pursue it → progress update → what's next → CTA inviting the audience to follow along.
**Secret sauce:** The dream must feel ambitious enough that the audience genuinely isn't sure you'll pull it off. Share vulnerable moments about whether you'll make it — it should feel personal, not like a project announcement.

Templates:
1. INTRODUCE DREAM: "I have [action] # times in my life, and ever since I was [X age] I have always dreamed of doing [dream]." / "For as long as I can remember, I've wanted to [dream]. It's something I think about every [day/week/month]."
2. PURSUIT OF DREAM: "After years of dreaming, on one random [weekday] I [realization/life event] and decided it was now or never so I started [action]. I won't lie, I was [vulnerable feeling] but I did it anyway." / "Then [X weeks/months] ago, I finally stopped talking about it and [first action]. I was terrified, but I knew if I didn't start now, I never would."
3. PROGRESS UPDATE: "So far I have already [action/actions], and now I am currently/about to start [action] which involves [step]. If I want to achieve [dream result] by [age/date] I need to complete this in [timeframe]." / "Here's where I'm at: I've [progress]. Next I need to [next step]. The deadline is [date]."
4. CTA: "Want to see if I can get [action] done on time? [CTA]" / "I'm going to document the entire thing. Follow along and hold me accountable."

### 6. Challenge
**Use when:** the creator is completing (or completed) a bounded mission with defined rules and a timeframe — vlog meets DIY.
**Structure:** Hook → establish the challenge's context → plot arc with conflicts/obstacles → resolution or cliffhanger.
**Secret sauce:** Make the objective extremely grand, and add high-intensity complications along the way.

Templates:
1. HOOK: "I'm going to [challenge] in [timeframe]. Here's what happened." / "Is it possible to [ambitious goal] starting from [starting point]? I tried it." / "I gave myself [X days] to [challenge]. No shortcuts."
2. RULES/SETUP: "The rules: [rule 1], [rule 2], and [rule 3]." / "Starting point: [baseline]. Goal: [target]. Deadline: [date]."
3. THE JOURNEY: "Day [X]: [progress/setback]. Things were [going well/falling apart] because [reason]." / "Then [complication] happened and I had to [pivot]. I almost [near-failure moment]." / "By day [X] I was only at [progress]. I needed to [remaining milestone] in [remaining time]."
4. RESOLUTION OR CLIFFHANGER: "Final result: [outcome]. I went from [start] to [end] in [timeframe]." / "And with [X hours] left... [cliffhanger]. Follow to see part 2."

### 7. Win (Victory Announcement)
**Use when:** announcing and celebrating a specific achievement — not teaching how you did it (that's format #2) and not a general life update (that's format #9).
**Structure:** Hook → state the win with proof → brief emotional context → (optional) acknowledgment of the journey/sacrifice → (optional) CTA.
**Secret sauce:** Works best with undeniable, visual proof (screenshot, notification, physical result). Keep it short and spontaneous-feeling.

Templates:
1. HOOK: "It finally happened: [win]." / "We just [achievement] and I'm still in shock." / "This is the moment I [milestone] — let me show you."
2. PROOF: "Look at this: [proof]." / "Here's the screenshot: [proof]. [X months/years] ago this number was [old number]." (or no words, just show it)
3. EMOTIONAL BEAT: "You have no idea how long I've worked for this. [X months/years] of [sacrifice/effort]." / "I almost quit [when/why]. This is why I didn't." / "This was the goal I set [X months/years] ago when I was [previous state]. It's real now."
4. ACKNOWLEDGMENT (optional): "From [before] to [after]. Wild." / "None of this happens without [key factor]."
5. CTA (optional): "Next goal: [next milestone]. Follow to watch." / "If you're working towards [similar goal], keep going. It's worth it."

### 8. Day In The Life
**Use when:** showing a typical day to build affinity for the creator's personality or lifestyle.
**Structure:** Hook → establish context (character, scene, objective) → introduce a complication/tension → break down the solve or thought process → (optional) CTA.
**Secret sauce:** The goal is making the viewer like the creator's personality or want their life — show something unexpected that breaks the audience's assumptions about how the day would go.

Templates:
1. HOOK: "A day in the life of a [age] year old [role/identity]." / "What a typical [weekday] looks like when you [lifestyle detail]." / "This is what it actually looks like to [aspiration]."
2. MORNING: "I start every day with [routine/habit] because [reason]." / "First thing: [action]. Most people don't know I [unexpected detail]."
3. CORE WORK: "Today's biggest priority is [task/objective]. The challenge is [complication]." / "Midway through [task], [problem] happened so I had to [pivot]."
4. RESOLUTION/WIND DOWN: "End of day. Here's what I got done: [summary]. Tomorrow I need to [next priority]." / "This is what [lifestyle] actually looks like. It's not perfect, but it's [honest feeling]."

### 9. Personal Update
**Use when:** sharing a life or mission update — for community-building, not authority-building.
**Structure:** Hook → context on the situation → the update (with tension/conflict) → rationale for the decision → (optional) CTA.
**Secret sauce:** Let people see behind the curtain — this format builds community, not credibility.

Templates:
1. HOOK: "I need to tell you something: [teaser]." / "Something big just changed in my [life/business/brand] and I want to explain why." / "I've been keeping this quiet for [X weeks/months], but it's time."
2. CONTEXT: "For the past [X months/years] I've been [previous state/plan]." / "You might remember that I was [previous situation]. Well..."
3. THE UPDATE: "As of [date/recently], I'm [the change]. Here's why." / "I've decided to [decision]. It wasn't easy because [tension/conflict]."
4. RATIONALE: "The reason is simple: [core reason]." / "I realized that [insight], and once I saw it I couldn't un-see it." / "At the end of the day, [value/priority] matters more to me than [tradeoff]."

### 10. Lesson From Others (Mentor Story)
**Use when:** the story belongs to someone else (mentor, client, peer, public figure) and the creator is the narrator/student, not the hero.
**Structure:** Hook → introduce the person and why they matter → describe their situation/struggle vividly → reveal the lesson → show how the creator applied it and what changed → (optional) CTA.
**Secret sauce:** Position the creator as a student/curator, not a self-promoter. Name the person if possible, make them feel like a real character, and land the lesson as a direct quote or vivid moment rather than a generic takeaway.

Templates:
1. HOOK: "I learned something powerful from [person/role]." / "Here's the best advice [figure] ever gave me." / "[Person] told me one thing that completely changed how I [area]."
2. SITUATION: "They were struggling with [problem]." / "At the time, they were [situation]." / "When I met them, they had just [event/context]."
3. THE LESSON: "They told me: '[golden lesson/quote].'" / "Their advice was simple: [lesson]. And it changed everything." / "The one thing they said that I'll never forget: '[quote].'"
4. APPLICATION: "When I applied it, I [result]." / "I took that advice and [action]. Within [timeframe], I [outcome]." / "Following that advice, I went from [before] to [after]."
5. CTA: "This one lesson can change everything for you too. [CTA]" / "If this resonated, save it. You'll need it on a hard day." / "Who's the person that taught you the most? Comment below."

## Notes
- All 10 formats are written from the creator's 1st person POV.
- "Optional" beats (usually the CTA) can be dropped if they don't fit the platform or the creator's style.
- These are starting skeletons, not finished copy — always replace bracketed placeholders with real, specific details rather than leaving generic language.
