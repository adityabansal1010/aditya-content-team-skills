# LinkedIn Audience Research

Produce deep, specific customer research that grounds LinkedIn content and positioning in the
audience's real frustrations, desires, language, and objections — not generic assumptions.

## Step 0: Whose audience is this for?

Ask up front: is this research for the ghostwriter's own audience, or for a client's audience?

Either way, gather the same four inputs before generating anything:

1. **Target audience** — who specifically they're trying to reach
2. **Top goal** — the single biggest outcome this audience is trying to achieve
3. **Product/service** — what the person (or their client) offers
4. **Industry** — the industry this all sits within

If this is for a client and the user doesn't have these answers yet, suggest they pull them from a
client interview, or note that the Content Pillars and Positioning Consultant skills in this plugin
can help surface some of this context too. Don't proceed with vague or placeholder inputs — push
for specificity, since generic inputs produce generic (useless) research.

## Generate the research

Once you have all four inputs, produce the following five sections. Be as specific, tangible, and
industry-relevant as possible — avoid generic statements that could apply to any audience in any
industry. Use the audience's actual language wherever you can infer it (the words they'd use to
describe their own problems), not marketing-speak paraphrases.

### 1. Frustrations, Wants, Dreams, Fears (FFGA Framework)

Produce a single table with 4 columns — **Frustration | Want | Dream | Fear** — containing:
- 10 rows pairing a frustration with its paired want (the opposite of that frustration)
- 10 rows pairing a dream with its paired fear (the opposite of that dream)

Make every entry highly specific to this industry and this business model — not generic workplace
frustrations or universal fears.

### 2. Questions & Problems

Produce four lists of 25 items each, all anchored to the audience's stated top goal:
- 25 questions this audience might have about achieving {top goal}
- 25 tactical problems they face trying to reach {top goal}
- 25 emotional problems they experience while pursuing {top goal}
- 25 mistakes they commonly make in the process

### 3. Misconceptions

10 misconceptions people commonly hold about the product/service described.

### 4. Objections

10 objections people have to buying the product/service described.

### 5. Audience Map

Map the audience segments most likely to be interested in this product/service, grouped into four
categories:
- **Demographics**
- **Psychographics**
- **Behaviors**
- **Buying triggers**

Use bullet lists or sub-tables under each category as appropriate.

## Formatting

- Format every section with a clear heading.
- Use tables for the FFGA framework (required) and wherever else a table makes the research easier
  to scan (e.g., the audience map).
- Use bullet or numbered lists for the questions/problems/mistakes, misconceptions, and objections.
- Keep all output in chat. Only save it as a document if the user explicitly asks — and if they do,
  this is a natural fit for a Word doc (the docx skill) or a structured spreadsheet (the xlsx skill)
  depending on how they want to use it downstream.

## Reminders

- Push back on vague inputs before generating — "my audience is business owners" isn't specific
  enough to produce useful research. Ask for more detail on who exactly, and what they're trying to
  achieve.
- This research is meant to feed directly into the Content Pillars and Post Writer skills in this
  plugin — flag that connection to the user so they know what to do with it next.
