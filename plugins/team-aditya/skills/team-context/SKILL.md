# Team Context

This is the orientation layer. Read it before any writing or editorial task so you are working with the right voice, the right client, and the right bar. It carries no strategy or commercial detail beyond what a writer needs to do the work.

**Run `who-is-working` first.** This account is shared, so establish which team member is in the session before anything else — it sets tone, defaults, and what you volunteer. This file tells you the standards; that one tells you who you're talking to.

## The standards every deliverable clears

These are non-negotiable and they apply to client work and to Aditya's own content equally.

1. **The substitution test.** Could this exact piece have been published by anyone else in the client's industry? If yes, it has failed and needs rewriting, not polishing. Specificity is the product.
2. **Voice is evidenced, not asserted.** "It's their voice" is a claim you have to back with a real post, a transcript line, or a documented voice profile. If you cannot point to evidence that they write that way, you are writing in your own voice and calling it theirs.
3. **Facts before style.** A wrong number published under a client's name outranks every stylistic concern. The audience includes people who know the real figure. Verify, cite, and soften anything unconfirmed.
4. **The house hard-bans.** No em dashes or en dashes. No emojis. No bold in the body. No AI openers. These apply to every draft unless a client's real writing provably does otherwise, and that exception has to be documented, not assumed.
5. **Nothing invented.** No fabricated stories, numbers, clients, results, or experiences, for any client, ever. If the material isn't there, ask for it.

## How the skills fit together

- **Voice target first, then the floor.** A voice skill (`aditya-voice`, `vivek-upavise`, `raag-voice`, or `lgos-client-content` for a client without a dedicated profile) sets what the writing should sound like. `signal-voice-guard` then enforces the anti-AI floor and audits the result. Load both, in that order.
- **`signal-editor`** is the judgment pass above the guard: scorecard, loop audit, substitution test, and a publish / revise / kill call. Run it when a draft is complete, paired with the relevant voice skill.
- **`li-writing-coach`, `storytelling-formats`, the `lgos-*` skills, and the `brandbuilder-*` skills** are the craft library: hooks, post formats, story structures, content pillars, audience research, positioning.
- **`signal-lead-engine` and `signal-outreach-craft`** are for new business, not client delivery. **`signal-inbound-funnel`** covers lead magnets and funnels, whether for Signal or sold to a client as a tier above ghostwriting.

## What to do when you are unsure

Ask. A question costs a message; a draft in the wrong voice, or one that invents a number under a client's name, costs the client's credibility. Specifically, ask rather than guess when: you don't know whose voice a piece is in, the client has no documented voice profile, a claim in the draft isn't traceable to source material, or the engagement scope isn't clear from the brief.
