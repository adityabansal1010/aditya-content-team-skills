# LinkedIn Hook Formats

There are 11 hook formats that work best for LinkedIn content. Every hook must follow these rules:

## Hook Writing Rules
- Length: Use 2 short & punchy single-sentence paragraphs with a line break between them, OR a 2–3-line paragraph under 140 characters
- The last sentence in a hook should end with a colon ":" if appropriate
- No formatting or headers
- No hashtags

---

## Format #1: Most Powerful

Uses a strong declarative statement to create an open loop. The parenthetical adds credibility or heightens the pain/benefit. The reader must click "See More" to find what the "Most Powerful" thing is.

**Examples:**
```
The single most powerful habit for personal growth:
(I've done this every morning for 5 years)
```
```
The most powerful reflection you can do:
(I've done this every month for 5 years)
```
```
The highest leverage habit you can build:
(This might surprise you)
```
```
The secret to writing online:
(It's not your hooks)
```

---

## Format #2: Big Number vs Small Number

Contrasts a large outcome with a surprisingly small investment (money, time, headcount, etc.). Suggests massive results from minimal effort. Uses social proof and aspirational appeal.

**Examples:**
```
In 5 years, my little business has generated $15,000,000.
It runs on just $8,215/month.
```
```
In the last 5 years, 3 small mindset shifts helped me lose 100 pounds and write every single morning:
```
```
These 7 small (but powerful) habits helped me quit my Wall Street job and generate $15,000,000 on the internet:
```

---

## Format #3: The Call Out

Directly addresses the intended audience using a command + a specific actionable number. Offers ready-to-use advice that can be immediately applied.

**Examples:**
```
Writers:
Stick these 19 powerful writing tips to your desk:
```

---

## Format #4: The Secret

Combines impressive proof with a cliffhanger question. Creates a curiosity gap — promising to reveal the key to an extraordinary result. The target audience is called out in the first line.

**Examples:**
```
Over the last 10 years, I've made $10,000,000+ as a writer.
The secret?
```
```
I'm not a "genius" writer.
But writing online made me a millionaire:
My secret?
```
```
The people you look up to are nothing special.
The only difference?
```

---

## Format #5: The Oddly Ignored

Positions the concept as contrarian and overlooked. Uses superlatives ("most underrated," "highest-leverage") and time-limited relevance (e.g., a year). Appeals to competitive advantage and FOMO.

**Examples:**
```
The most underrated, oddly-ignored, yet highest-leverage skill in 2025:
Lean Writing.
```
```
The most misunderstood, misused, yet highest-leverage writing skill of 2025:
Copywriting.
```

---

## Format #6: The Struggle

Uses a relatable struggle followed by transformation — a before-and-after narrative with specific proof. Creates empathy and aspiration. Readers want proven methods from someone who overcame what they're currently facing.

**Examples:**
```
I struggled for *years* to build a daily writing habit.
But now, I write for 90 minutes every single day:
```
```
My first book took me 4 years to write.
But now I can write an entire book in 30 days.
```
```
I'm not surprised I became a millionaire by my early 30s.
Because I put myself through hell in my 20s:
```

---

## Format #7: How To

Addresses a fear or desired benefit, promises a specific solution within a timeframe. The parenthetical acknowledges objections OR "twists the knife" by heightening the pain or benefit.

**Examples:**
```
How to generate 100 ideas to write about in 30 minutes (even if you think you have nothing interesting to say):
```
```
9 reasons why I'm writing every day in 2025 (this is one of the most powerful habits you can build):
```
```
How I generate 20 writing ideas every morning in under 5 minutes—without any "additional" work:
```
```
I studied the writing routine of 500 legendary writers. Anyone can be a great writer—if you follow these 6 simple habits:
```

---

## Format #8: Belief Flip

Takes a common limiting belief and contrasts it with shocking contradictory evidence from the writer's own career. Dismantles myths, inspires the audience to reconsider their beliefs.

**Examples:**
```
I used to think all writers died broke.
But I've generated $15,000,000 as a writer:
```

---

## Format #9: The List

Creates a curiosity gap by listing 3+ items about a specific topic. Because LinkedIn cuts off at 3 lines, the list creates an open loop — readers want to see everything else in the list. Uses tangible language with active verbs, numbers, and short punchy points.

**Examples:**
```
At 22, I was:
• 280 pounds
• Drinking 40 beers a week
• Tens of thousands in debt

My life was a mess.
But reading 7 core beliefs every morning rewired my mindset (and changed my life):
```
```
Daily routine 6 years ago:
1. Screeching 5AM alarm
2. Immediately caffeinate
3. Have a pathetic morning workout
4. Work under fluorescent lights for 12 hours
5. Take freezing cold NYC subway home
6. Grab a 6 pack and sit on the couch
7. Smile & say I'm living the dream

But now?
```
```
I've spent 10,000+ hours ghostwriting for:
• Startup founders
• Olympic athletes
• NYT best-selling authors
```
```
I've generated over $5,000,000 ghostwriting.
• No fancy website
• No expensive tools
• No complex funnels

Just a simple cold outreach strategy.
```

---

## Format #10: The Crazy Part

Establishes credibility with a specific impressive achievement, then uses "the crazy part?" to create a curiosity gap. Combines social proof with counterintuitive intrigue. Readers want surprising secrets from people ahead of them on the journey.

**Examples:**
```
At 26, I quit my full-time job as a copywriter. But 60 days later, I hit $20k/month as a ghostwriter. The crazy part?
```
```
In 5 years, I wrote for 300+ industry leaders and made over $3,000,000 as a ghostwriter. But the crazy part?
```

---

## Format #11: Everyone Should

Uses universal empowerment ("everyone should") combined with a specific desirable outcome. The second line adds relatable pain points. Creates inclusive motivation and FOMO — making success seem both achievable and necessary.

**Examples:**
```
Everyone should have a digital business making $10k/month.
Sadly, most overthink, never act, and waste years.
```
```
Everyone should have a writing business that makes at least $10k/month.
```
