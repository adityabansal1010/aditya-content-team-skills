# Voice Archetypes

The Voice Remixer rewrites any piece of text in 5 distinct archetypes to help ghostwriters identify, study, and replicate a client's natural writing voice.

---

## The 5 Archetypes

### 1. The Storyteller
Storytellers love dates, times, locations, and real-life examples of what they've learned. They anchor abstract ideas in concrete moments.

Signal phrases: "In 1999…", "The first time I…", "A few years ago…", "Back when I was…", "I remember the day…"

### 2. The Opinionator
Opinionators emphasize strong opinions on topics. They use adverbs for emphasis and make declarative statements with conviction.

Signal phrases: "There's a reason why…", "It's unbelievable how…", "It's abundantly clear that…", "Never", "Always", "Really", "Rarely", "Honestly"

### 3. The Fact Presenter
Fact Presenters back up their opinions with memorable stats, research-backed stories, or cited studies. They give readers evidence, not just assertions.

Signal phrases: Stats, Studies, Research papers, Surveys — "According to…", "Research shows…", "A 2024 study found…"

### 4. The Frameworker
Frameworkers give readers something actionable — a clear path from point A to point B. They think in systems and always name their frameworks.

Signal phrases: "How-tos", "A Proven Process…", "A Timeless Framework…", "A Simple Checklist…", "The [Name] Method", "Here's the 3-step system:"

### 5. The F-Bomber
F-Bombers are abrasive and push boundaries with brash, unfiltered language. They curse, rant, and use sarcasm. High-energy, no-filter writing.

Signal phrases: Cursing, rants, blunt declarations, sarcasm, rhetorical punches

## Output Format

Always output the Voice Remixer results as a table with two columns:

| Tone | Rewritten sentence |
|------|-------------------|
| Original voice | [original text] |
| The Storyteller | [rewritten] |
| The Opinionator | [rewritten] |
| The Fact Presenter | [rewritten] |
| The Frameworker | [rewritten] |
| The F-Bomber | [rewritten] |

The first row is always the original text labeled "Original voice."

---

## How to Use for Client Voice Matching

1. Ask the client (or find samples of) their existing writing — LinkedIn posts, emails, bios, anything they've written themselves
2. Run `/pgali-remix-voice` on a representative sample
3. Identify which 1–2 archetypes dominate their natural output
4. When ghostwriting for them, write primarily in those archetypes
5. Use the other archetypes sparingly for variety and contrast
