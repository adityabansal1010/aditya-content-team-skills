# LinkedIn Post Writing Framework

## The 5 Content Styles

Every LinkedIn post uses one of these 5 styles as its content type:

1. **Steps** — A sequential process readers can follow
2. **Stats** — Data-backed insights and research-driven points
3. **Mistakes** — Common errors to avoid (with solutions)
4. **Lessons** — Hard-won insights from experience
5. **Examples** — Real-world case studies or illustrations

The chosen style should be consistently mirrored in all headers throughout the post (e.g., "Step 1...", "Mistake 1...", "Lesson 1...").

---

## The 5 Universal Formatting Rules

These rules apply to every LinkedIn post regardless of style.

---

### Rule #1: The First 200 Characters Matter Most

LinkedIn shows ~200 characters before the "See More" prompt. These are the most important words — they hook readers and signal to the algorithm (via click-throughs) that a post is worth distributing.

The first 200 characters must be both a hook AND a cliffhanger. The best opening hooks are:
- A strong, declarative statement
- A thought-provoking question
- A controversial opinion
- A weird, unique insight, stat, or data point
- A meaningful moment-in-time in history

The ending of those first 200 characters should leave readers needing to know the next line.

---

### Rule #2: Tangible, Specific, Actionable, Skimmable Headers

LinkedIn doesn't support bold headers, so signal headers visually using:
- `Item #1: Header sentence here.`
- `1/ Header sentence here.`
- `1. Header sentence here.`

Header writing rules:
- Written in Sentence Style — first word capitalized, everything else lowercase, punctuation at end
- Each header should deliver a complete, standalone tactical point — readers should extract 80% of the value just by reading headers
- Headers are section dividers AND mini-promises that deliver on the hook's claim
- More headers = less room per section; fewer headers = more room to go deep. Choose based on whether the value is in the list itself or in the explanation

---

### Rule #3: Sell-The-Reader Introductions (200–400 characters)

The introduction immediately follows the hook (after "See More" is clicked). Its job is to sell readers on continuing. Structure it in 4 blocks:

**Block 1 — Opening hook:** The first 200 characters (already written)

**Block 2 — The promise:** 3–5 sentences OR a bulleted list of 3–5 points. Educate readers on either:
- The problem (why it exists, negative outcomes of not solving it)
- The desired outcome (why it's valuable, consequences of not achieving it)

**Block 3 — The solution:** 1–2 sentences stating what you're going to help them with today

**Block 4 — Transition:** Short bridge into the first section. Examples:
- "Let's walk through each one."
- "Let's dive in."
- "So, with that in mind, let's [solve/achieve]."

**Example Introduction:**
```
Do you want to know the secret to landing your first ghostwriting client?

One of the biggest challenges I faced getting started was: how do I actually charge for my work and make money?

The truth is, it's not that hard.

It's just that no one tells you how to do it.

So today, I'm going to share the 3 ways to structure your next ghostwriting project—and when to use them to get paid a premium.

Let's walk through each one.
```

---

### Rule #4: Compress Maximum Tactical Value Into Each Section

Each section follows a What / How / Why structure (internal framework — do NOT label these in the output):

- **What (declarative opener):** The hook inside the section — the main point stated clearly
- **How (3–5 tactical pieces):** Tips, stats, steps, lessons, benefits, reasons, mistakes, examples, or questions
- **Why (closing insight):** One sentence — the benefit, insight, or motivating reason to act

**CRITICAL — Alternate section formats throughout the post:**
- Odd-numbered sections (1, 3, 5...): Use bulleted lists for the "How" content
- Even-numbered sections (2, 4, 6...): Use paragraph style for the "How" content
- Never use the same format twice in a row

---

### Rule #5: One-Sentence Paragraphs + Strategic Bullet Lists

LinkedIn is a skimming environment. Long blocks of text kill engagement.

**One-sentence paragraphs — always:**
- Every sentence that is its own thought gets its own line
- Never stack 2+ sentences into a single paragraph block
- Each line break creates breathing room and makes the post feel fast to read

**Use bulleted lists whenever you have:**
- A group of 3+ related items, tips, tools, or examples
- A before/after contrast or comparison
- A series of steps that don't require deep explanation
- Any list where prose would create a wall of text

**What this looks like in practice:**

Instead of:
```
One of the biggest challenges I faced getting started was how to charge for my work. The truth is, it's not that hard. It's just that no one tells you how to do it.
```

Write it as:
```
One of the biggest challenges I faced getting started was: how do I charge for my work?

The truth is, it's not that hard.

It's just that no one tells you how to do it.
```

This rule applies to every part of the post — hook, introduction, section bodies, and conclusion.

## Target Section Lengths (for a ~2,800 character post)

| Section | Target Length |
|---------|--------------|
| Hook (first 200 chars) | ~200 characters |
| Introduction | ~300–400 characters |
| Each main section | ~300–450 characters max |
| Conclusion | ~100–200 characters |

**CHARACTER LIMIT IS NON-NEGOTIABLE: Stay under 2,800 characters total.**

If over the limit, compress in this order:
1. Remove redundant phrases and filler words
2. Shorten bullet points to essential elements only
3. Condense paragraph sections while keeping key tactical details
4. Reduce number of examples/bullets per section if necessary

---

## Idea Generation Process

Before writing the post, generate content ideas:

1. Generate 3 potential content ideas (as Headlines) for each of the 5 styles = 15 ideas total
2. Evaluate all 15 against LinkedIn's demographics and viral potential
3. Select the 1 idea most likely to perform
4. Write that idea as a full post following all 5 formatting rules
