# Upavise — Founder Voice & Content System (Vivek instance)

This skill does two jobs:

1. **Write content that sounds like Vivek Ravisankar** and serves HackerRank's two goals.
2. **Provide a repeatable founder-voice methodology** that can be re-run for any founder client (see "Repurposing" at the bottom).

Companion files, load before drafting:
- `references/vivek-voice-profile.md` — the condensed voice profile. **Always load this before writing anything for Vivek.**
- `references/source-quote-bank.md` — the full extraction from five podcast transcripts: pillars, verified quotes, stories with timestamps, spiky POVs. Load when you need exact quotes, story beats, or new material.

---

## The two goals and the two tiers

HackerRank's stated goals:
- **Public goal:** maximize views (reach).
- **Real goal (primary metric):** peer connects — CTOs, founders, and industry peers who reach out, mention the posts at events, or engage directly.

Every piece of content is assigned a tier BEFORE drafting:

**Tourist content** — general-niche, aimed at reach and follower growth. Broad founder/career/India/AI themes. Emotional stories, universal lessons. Judged on views, shares, non-follower reach.

**Purest content** — hyper-niche, aimed at resonance and peer acknowledgment. Hiring craft, engineering leadership, assessment design, live company experiments, intellectually honest takes. Judged on WHO engages: a comment from a CTO beats 10,000 impressions.

**Tier decision rule:** ask "who is the ideal single reader of this post?" If the answer is "anyone ambitious," it's tourist. If the answer is "a VP of Engineering at a 500-person company," it's purest.

**Default cadence: roughly 60% purest, 40% tourist.** Tourist earns the audience; purest converts it into the metric that matters. Never let a tourist-heavy week go by without a purest piece, because the peer-connect goal compounds slowly and dies fast.

## Drafting workflow

1. **Load** `references/vivek-voice-profile.md`.
2. **Assign the tier** and state it explicitly in the output.
3. **Source the material.** Every post must be anchored in something from the quote bank (a story, a number, a framework, a spiky POV) or in new raw material the client supplies (e.g., a quarterly feature release, an internal experiment). Never invent stories, numbers, or experiences for Vivek.
4. **Apply the pairing rule.** Every big claim gets a stat AND a human story within a breath of each other. A post with neither is not a Vivek post.
5. **Draft in the voice mix** (see profile: ~40% Frameworker, 30% Storyteller, 20% Fact Presenter, 10% Opinionator, 0% F-Bomber).
6. **Run the kill filter** (in the profile) line by line.
7. **Check source integrity.** If the idea originated with a podcast guest (Dohmke, Aravind, Khosla), it must be framed as "in conversation with X" or attributed — never presented as Vivek's original thought. His audience includes these exact people.

## Hard rules (Signal house rules + Vivek-specific)

- No em dashes in any drafted content.
- No hustle-bro vocabulary, no rage-bait, no attacks on people. Sharp edges go at ideas (the GPA filter, blind-copying Google), never at humans.
- No absolutist AI-timeline claims. Vivek speaks in probabilities ("I'm 50/50 on this").
- Attribution honesty: if Vivek credited a line to someone ("maybe it was Ben Horowitz, I don't remember"), the post keeps the attribution, fuzziness included. It's part of his credibility.
- Every stat must exist in the quote bank or be supplied by the client. No invented numbers.
- Verbatim quotes from transcripts get spot-checked against the video before publishing (transcripts are auto-generated; the Gobinath pod passes through Tamil translation).

## Format playbooks

### LinkedIn post (primary format)
Structure: **Hook (story-in-motion or counterintuitive claim) → the scene or data → the frame ("There are three things here" / named principle) → the turn (what he changed or learned) → one-line close, often self-deprecating or a question to peers.**
- Open in the middle of a story or with the number, never with throat-clearing.
- Short paragraphs, mixed sentence lengths, punch endings ("We made 4,000 rupees.").
- Purest posts end with a peer-directed question ("How are your debriefs structured?") to drive the peer-connect metric. Tourist posts end on the emotional beat.

### X (Twitter)
- One idea per post. The punchline version of a LinkedIn post, or a standalone observation.
- Meme-shaped material: self-deprecating founder moments (the ₹4,000 Excel), the intern stories, "I deleted the slide."
- Threads only for frameworks (the three frames, NextGen hiring). Never thread a story that fits in one post.

### Short video / AI storytelling (HackerRank's second priority)
Script structure: **cold-open line (the hook sentence of the story) → 20–40 seconds of the story or analogy → the principle in one sentence → HackerRank tie-in only if organic.**
- Vivek's analogies are natively visual: the cockpit, the orchestra of agents, the needle in the haystack, the rubber band, the monkey on the branch. Lead video concepts with these.
- The Bangalore content division and quarterly feature releases are raw material: "what we shipped and why" videos in a founder's voice, not a corporate one.
- AI-assisted production is fine; AI-invented stories are not. Every video is anchored in a real story from the bank or a real company event.

### Newsletter (Tyler Denk / beehiiv model)
Casual, build-in-public, founder-brain-dump energy. Not corporate comms. Suggested recurring structure per issue:
1. **What we're building** — one quarterly feature or internal experiment, told with the reasoning and the messy parts (the superpower JD experiment is exactly this shape).
2. **What I'm thinking about** — one essay-length purest idea (hiring craft, human+AI, humans-as-a-service).
3. **One story** — from the bank or from the week, with the lesson.
- Voice check: would this read like an internal memo a smart friend forwarded you? If it reads like a press release, rewrite.
- The newsletter is the purest-tier home base; LinkedIn/X are distribution for it.

## Repurposing: founder-voice analysis for any client

To run this system for a new founder, execute this intake and produce a new profile reference file (keep this SKILL.md unchanged; swap the references):

1. **Collect 3–5 long-form sources** (podcast transcripts, talks, long interviews). Tag each source: is the founder the GUEST (their ideas) or the HOST (mostly others' ideas — only their framings are usable)?
2. **Build the repetition map.** Ideas the founder repeats across 2+ sources unprompted are the true core beliefs. These become the pillars. One-off remarks are supporting material.
3. **Extract into three asset types per pillar:** Principles (beliefs), Stories (dated, placed, named — the substitution-test alpha), Spiky POVs (contrarian positions). Attach verbatim quotes and timestamps to everything.
4. **Run the 5 Voice Archetypes analysis** (Storyteller / Opinionator / Fact Presenter / Frameworker / F-Bomber) and produce a working mix percentage, a verbal-habits list, an analogy library, and a kill filter ("would [founder] say this?").
5. **Map the client's goals to a two-tier content system** (reach tier vs. resonance tier), define the tier decision rule and cadence.
6. **Produce the highest-leverage combinations list:** story + number + principle combos that pass the substitution test (only this founder can post them).
7. Save the output as `references/[founder]-voice-profile.md` + `references/[founder]-quote-bank.md`.

The deliverable of an intake is always two files, mirroring the Vivek instance. Content drafting then follows the same workflow above.
