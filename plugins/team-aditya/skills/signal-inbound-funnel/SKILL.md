# Signal Inbound Funnel

An authority-led inbound system: content builds trust, a lead magnet captures the 95% of the market that isn't ready to buy today, and a landing page → sales page → email sequence turns that captured interest into booked calls. This is the inbound counterpart to `signal-lead-engine` (cold/warm outbound). Where the outbound engine goes and finds people, this system gets found and lets people self-select in.

**Source note:** this skill distills the framework from a Kurogo lead-magnet guide. The mechanics (structure, sequencing, checklists) are solid and worth using. The stats quoted in that guide (leads/month, conversion rates) are Kurogo's own marketing proof for their paid service — treat them as an existence proof that the model works, not as a benchmark to expect immediately.

## The core logic (why build this at all)

Only a small slice of any market is ready to buy right now — the people who already know their problem, know the solution, and DM after one good post. Everyone else needs touchpoints before they trust enough to book a call: repeated exposure, proof, and a reason to hand over contact info before they're sold. A lead magnet is what captures that slice while it's still forming an opinion, instead of losing it to whichever competitor's funnel it stumbles into next.

Three systems, three jobs. Don't let one page try to do two jobs — that's the single most common way a funnel underperforms:

1. **Capture** — the lead magnet + landing page. Job: get the download. Nothing else.
2. **Monetise** — the sales page. Job: convert the warmest people into a booked call or a direct sale.
3. **Nurture** — the email sequence. Job: keep addressing objections until the rest are ready.

## Part 1 — The lead magnet

A lead magnet is a sales asset, not a bribe for an email address. It has to do three things at once:

- Capture interest from someone who has the problem
- Solve one specific, painful problem, thoroughly
- Surface the *next* problem they now realize they have (this is what creates the next conversation)

**Bar to clear:** would someone be annoyed if they'd paid $200 for this and it was mediocre? If not, it isn't done. Free content that's genuinely better than a competitor's paid product is the actual moat here — most people won't put in this level of effort, which is exactly why it works for the ones who do.

**Structure (in this order — order matters as much as content):**

1. **Problem, Promise, Proof** — name the pain, promise the outcome, back it with evidence up front.
2. **Key belief shift** — what the reader currently (wrongly) believes, and what's actually true. Skip this and jump to tactics, and the guide reads as generic and gets ignored.
3. **Name your system** — give your method a name and a clear shape. This is what makes it feel proprietary instead of like recycled advice.
4. **Objection handling** — name the top 2-3 reasons someone talks themselves out of this, and take them apart before the reader gets the chance to use them.
5. **The value itself** — for each step: why it matters, what to actually do, what happens if they get it right.
6. **Proof it works** — specific numbers, named results, before/after. Vague proof is weak proof — always prefer a specific number over an adjective.
7. **New problem** — the thing they'll realize they still need help with once this one's solved.
8. **Call to action** — the next step, tied directly to the new problem just raised.

## Part 2 — Landing page

Only job: get the download. Not to sell the offer, not to explain the whole philosophy.

- Pre-header naming exactly who this is for and that it's free
- Headline: the result, minus the pain they're avoiding ("X, without Y")
- 3–5 named "secrets"/reasons this guide has the answer — not a table of contents
- Proof (testimonials/results) that people like them got value
- About you — 3–4 credibility facts, not a full bio
- Contact form

## Part 3 — Sales page

Where the download redirects to. This person just self-selected as having the problem — this page's only job is to sell the *next step* (a call for anything $1,000+, direct purchase for low-ticket).

- Confirm the resource is theirs, then immediately name the next step
- What happens on the call (removes "I don't know what I'm signing up for")
- Problem headline — make them feel understood, not sold to
- Outcome + sub-results
- Credibility — who you are, what you've done, numbers
- Process — how you actually work, as steps
- Before vs after — 3-10 concrete transformations
- Objections — name the top fears directly and answer them in text, don't wait for the call
- Testimonials, FAQ, guarantee, bonuses — proof first, mechanics last

## Part 4 — Email nurture sequence

Most people don't book after one touchpoint. That's not a lost lead, it's someone who needs more contact before converting — the sequence is how you keep earning it.

- **Email 1:** deliver the lead magnet, plainly. "You asked for this. Here it is. It'll help you X."
- **Ongoing emails — one objection, one proof point, per email.** List every objection and false belief the audience has, then write one email per belief: name it, correct it, back the correction with a real result. Never bundle multiple objections into one email — it dilutes the shift you're going for.
- **Cadence:** daily for the first stretch to close near-term objections, then weekly to stay top-of-mind until the reader is ready. Don't let it go silent — a lead magnet with no follow-up is a lead you already earned and then let go cold.

## Common failure modes (check against these before shipping)

- Lead magnet isn't valuable enough (would-be-annoyed-if-paid-for test fails)
- Value undersold in the copy — a 9/10 guide with a 5/10 headline never gets read
- Built once, promoted once, then forgotten — promotion is ongoing, not a launch event
- One page trying to do capture + monetise + nurture at once
- No email sequence after the download — the objections just sit there unaddressed
- Targeting "founders" instead of "agency founders doing $30-100k/month stuck on referrals" — vague targeting produces vague, forgettable copy
- Proof without specifics — names, numbers, timeframes beat adjectives every time

## The two ways this gets used

Two separate uses — don't conflate them:

**1. Signal's own inbound engine.** The pipeline runs primarily on outbound (the two-loop Signal Sales Engine) plus existing relationships. What's missing is an asset that captures the founders and VC partners who see the content, aren't ready to buy today, and would opt into something like "The Ghostwriter's Guide to Founder-Led Content ROI" or a positioning/voice-audit assessment. Built once, this runs underneath the outbound engine instead of replacing it: outbound reaches the 5% directly, this captures a slice of the other 95% who read the posts but never DM.

**2. A packaged service for clients.** Clients who are themselves trying to build authority-led inbound demand around a founder's personal brand can buy this as a tier above straight LinkedIn ghostwriting. Lead magnet, landing and sales page copy, nurture sequence: it's the same "growth layer on top of content production" positioning, extended past posts into the funnel that turns readers into booked calls. Check the client's current scope before proposing it.

When asked to actually build one of these (a lead magnet, a landing page, a sales page, or a sequence), ask which of the two tracks it's for, then apply the relevant structure above, drafted in the target's actual voice (Aditya's own via `aditya-voice`/`signal-voice-guard` for track 1, the client's established voice profile for track 2).
